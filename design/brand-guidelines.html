01 / The brand
Built like the firm.
Alares is a healthcare architecture and engineering practice. Since 2007 we have designed MRI suites, oncology renovations, dental clinics, and campus utility plants for the VA, federal agencies, and private healthcare clients, environments where precision isn't a preference, it's a requirement. The brand should feel the way the work does: exact, calm, and dependable.
Where it comes from.
Our approach reflects our founder, Don Maggioli, a West Point graduate and former officer of the Green Berets. The firm was built on the leadership training and high standards he carried from those experiences: plan thoroughly, move decisively, own the outcome.
Today that discipline shows up as a "can-do" attitude on every project, from a 23-building fire alarm modernization to a 2,200-foot geothermal test well.
"Always achieve our clients' objectives in a swift manner, without sacrificing quality for speed."

02 / Voice & tone
Say it straight.
Alares writes the way its engineers report: short sentences, active voice, real numbers. The work is impressive. The words don't need to be.
Direct
Lead with what was done and for whom. Cut every word that isn't working.
Technical, plainly said
Use real specs, NFPA, Revit, seismic bracing, but explain them like a colleague, not a brochure.
Confident, not salesy
Let scope, budget, and outcomes carry the argument. No superlatives, no buzzwords.
Steady under pressure
Calm and can-do, even when writing about risk, change orders, or active hospital sites.
In practice
✓ Write
"We replaced 500 feet of steam main beneath an active hospital."
"Pre-construction surveys eliminated change-order risk."
✕ Don't write
"We leveraged innovative solutions to deliver transformational infrastructure outcomes."
"Our best-in-class methodology maximizes stakeholder synergies."
House conventions
—Headlines are sentence case and end with a period: "Eight disciplines, one team."
—ALL CAPS is reserved for labels, eyebrows, and the logo descriptor, never for headlines or body copy.
—Name the client and the place: "Albany VAMC, NY." Specificity is credibility.
—The firm is "Alares" in running text; "Alares Architects & Engineers" on first formal reference and legal use.
03 / The logo
The mark of the firm.
The lockup pairs a three-layer wing with the Alares wordmark. The wing, lift layered like plan sheets, carries the secondary blues; the wordmark holds Alares Blue; the descriptor sits in Alares Red. Always reproduce it from supplied artwork.
1 2 3
1 · Wing emblem, three layers in Sky, Bright, and Deep Blue. Never redraw or reorder the layers.
2 · Wordmark, "ALARES" in Alares Blue. Fixed letterforms; never retype.
3 · Descriptor, "ARCHITECTS & ENGINEERS" in Alares Red, uppercase, width-matched to the wordmark.
Clear space
X X X X
Keep a margin of at least X = the height of the wing emblem clear on all sides. Nothing enters this zone, no type, rules, photos, or page edges.
Minimum size
Lockup · 150 px / 1.5 in wide min
Emblem alone · 24 px / 0.25 in min
Below minimum size the descriptor fails, so use the emblem alone (favicons, app icons, drawing stamps).

Rules of use
—Body text is always Ink or Slate on white or Mist. Colored body text is reserved for links.
—Alares Red stays under ~5% of any layout: the descriptor, one key stat, an alert. Never red headlines, never red backgrounds behind body copy.
—Never set red on blue or blue on red, the logo is the only place the two meet, separated by white.
—Charts and diagrams use the blue ramp in order, Alares, Deep, Bright, Sky, with red only to flag the exception.
—Prefer the secondary blues over opacity tints; tints print unpredictably.
—Bright and Sky Blue are accents and fills, not text colors on white.
05 / Typography
One typeface: Bahnschrift.

05 / Typography, rules
Setting it right.
—No italics. Bahnschrift has none. Emphasize with SemiBold or Alares Blue, never faux-slanted type.
—Headlines: sentence case, terminal period, tight leading (1.05–1.15), letter-spacing −0.5 px at display sizes.
—Caps with +0.2 em tracking for eyebrows and labels only. Never track body copy.
—Dense tables and schedules may use Bahnschrift's SemiCondensed width axis; never condense manually.
—Minimums: 10 pt body in print, 14 px on screen, 24 px on 1920×1080 slides.
Availability
Bahnschrift ships with Windows 10 (1709) and later, no license needed for internal documents. It is not preinstalled on macOS, iOS, or Android, so anything leaving the firm needs the fallback stack.
Fallback stack
Web & cross-platform: Barlow Semi Condensed (Google Fonts), the closest open DIN relative. Email and Office templates: Arial.

06 / Graphic language
The wing supplies the only curve.
Layouts are rectilinear, square corners, hairline rules, generous white space, numbered everything. Against that grid, the wing emblem is the one expressive element. Use it deliberately and sparingly.
Watermark on dark.
White emblem at 8–12% opacity, cropped off a corner. One per composition.
Edge crop on light.
Full-color emblem at 100%, large and cropped, section dividers, covers. Min 200 px.
—Crop from a corner or edge; never rotate, mirror, tile, or use the wing as a repeating pattern.
—Never set text over the emblem's layered area at small sizes, keep type on clear field.
Index numbering
Disciplines / 01 / 08
Sections, projects, and figures are numbered with two digits: current / total. Eyebrow style, caps, +0.2 em, Alares Blue. It reads like a sheet index because it is one.
Rules, captions & corners
—Hairlines are 1 px Hairline gray. Square corners everywhere, no rounded containers, no pill buttons.
—Photo captions follow the sheet format: 02 / VA Dental Clinic / treatment, 8–9 pt, Slate.
—Accent moves: a 3 px Alares Blue top border on tiles; a single red figure in a field of blue. Nothing else.
07 / Imagery
Show the real work.
Every image is a project Alares actually built, clinical interiors, mechanical rooms, job sites. Photograph them with the same discipline as the drawings: corrected verticals, honest light, order in the frame.
01 / Outpatient clinic / reception
02 / Mechanical / air handler
03 / VA Dental Clinic / check-in
—Straight-on or two-point perspective; verticals corrected, horizons level.
—Natural or ambient light, neutral white balance. No HDR halos, no filters.
—Clinical spaces clean and ready; mechanical rooms shot as heroes, symmetry, order, labeled systems.
—People appear working, candid, in proper PPE.
✓ Use
Full-color photography. For text legibility, an Ink scrim up to 40% or a white plate, nothing else on top of photos.
✕ Avoid
Stock metaphors, handshakes, globes, hard-hat clip art. Tilted horizons, fisheye distortion, blue-wash overlays, heavy vignettes.
